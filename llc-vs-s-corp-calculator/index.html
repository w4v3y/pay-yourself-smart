Where the S-Corp starts paying off

Savings at each profit level, keeping your salary at the same share of profit and your running costs as entered.

How this is calculated

  • LLC: 15.3% self-employment tax on 92.35% of profit, with the Social Security part capped at the 2026 wage base of $184,500. Half of it is deducted before income tax.
  • S-Corp: 15.3% FICA on your salary only (half paid by the company, half withheld), plus federal unemployment tax of 0.6% on the first $7,000.
  • The remaining profit flows to you on Schedule K-1 without payroll tax, after the company deducts its payroll taxes and running costs.
  • QBI deduction: 20% of business income, limited to 20% of taxable income. Above $201,750 ($403,500 joint) it phases in the W-2 wage limit, and service businesses phase out over the next $75,000 ($150,000 joint).
  • 2026 brackets and standard deduction ($16,100 single, $32,200 joint) from IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32.

What it leaves out

  • State income tax, which can raise or lower the result in either direction.
  • Health insurance, retirement contributions and other deductions that change with entity type.
  • The 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax threshold when other wages exist, and capital gains.
  • Head of household and married filing separately status.

A lower salary raises the estimate, but it also raises audit risk. Choose the salary first, then look at the savings.

Educational estimate only, not tax, legal or accounting advice. Results depend on your state, your full return and how the IRS views your salary. Talk to a CPA or Enrolled Agent before filing Form 2553.
